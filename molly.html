How it's calculated. Each account compounds monthly at the annual rate you set. Contributions are added at each month's end. Both accounts use the same growth rate.

Taxes aren't modeled. The 401(k) grows tax-deferred, but the taxable account's real return would be lower after taxes on dividends and gains — treat its line as a gross, before-tax figure.

Other things to remember. 401(k) contributions are capped by the IRS each year. Real markets don't return a smooth rate — they lurch, and the order of good and bad years matters. This is a projection, not a prediction, and not financial advice.
